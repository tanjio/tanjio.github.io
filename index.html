About
I research how organizations actually work, then help fix what's not working.

My core question has always been the same: what is actually going on with people in this organization, and what should the leadership do about it? I've asked that at Zalando (15,000 employees, 25 markets), at UN Women in Kyrgyzstan, at USAID in Ethiopia, and in research settings at University of Zagreb and Columbia University. The environments were very different, but the discipline was the same.

I build and run employee listening programs, lead organizational diagnostics, design employee experience strategy with KPIs, and translate workforce data into something senior leaders can act on. I've done this in fast-scaling tech, in international development, and in academia. I am most useful when the data exists but organizations struggle to turn it into impactful decisions, or when the policy exists but employees are not using it as designed.

I'm based in Zagreb, Croatia and Scottsdale, AZ. I've also lived in New York City, Burlington, Vermont; Berlin, Germany; Budapest, Hungary; Bishkek, Kyrgyzstan and Addis Ababa, Ethiopia. I think in Croatian and English.

Writing & Speaking

Things I've researched, spoken about, and written up. For practitioners.

Fireside Chat · UNICEF CSR Academy · April 2026

Family-friendly policy at scale: what actually works

Fireside chat at the UNICEF CSR Academy, April 2026. The real gap between having a policy and having a culture where people feel safe using it. I talked through what we did at Zalando: 30 focus groups before writing the hybrid work policy. The 3 diagnostic questions I use with every organization: is leadership visibly using the policy (e.g. flexible working hours)? Who is actually using it, and who isn't (is it only women using it, only new joiners etc.)? Who is structurally excluded by how the policy was designed (only office employees have the right for hybrid work, but what's offered to the factory and warehouse workers)? Why the parents' network was majority women, and what that data actually meant.

Corporate Culture · Employee Listening · Family Policy
Workshop · Wellbeing u Praksi · March 2026

Wellbeing in rhythm: the menstrual cycle as an employee effectiveness question

Workshop co-presented with Nadja Tobias, March 2026. We started with biology (the infradian rhythm, what it actually does to energy and work elan across 4 phases of the menstrual cycle) and ended up in organizational design. How can we design work so that menstruating people define their peak performance times? Whose biology is the default? The room was HR professionals who were curious about the topic of wellbeing at work.

Research · Academy of Management · Vancouver 2016

Same behavior, different penalty: networking and gender

Presented at the Academy of Management Annual Meeting, Vancouver, 2016. Identical networking behaviors evaluated differently depending on gender. Women penalized for things that read as ambition in men. The implications for performance review design are pretty direct, and most organizations are still ignoring them.

Research led for UNICEF Croatia, published 2019. Full cycle: scoping, data collection across government, corporate, and civil society stakeholders, analysis, policy recommendations.
